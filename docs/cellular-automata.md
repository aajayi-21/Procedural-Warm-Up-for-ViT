# Cellular Automata as Procedural Pretraining Data for Vision Transformers: Theory, Evidence, and Experimental-Design Considerations

## TL;DR
- The hypothesis is well-grounded: existing evidence (notably "Intelligence at the Edge of Chaos," ICLR 2025) shows transformers pretrained to predict cellular-automata (CA) dynamics acquire transferable computational priors, and the Shinnick et al. ViT "procedural warm-up" framework already explicitly names cellular automata as a candidate procedural source — so a masked-state CA pretraining axis is a natural, defensible extension.
- The single most important design principle is rule selection by complexity: across the literature, intermediate-to-high "edge of chaos" complexity (Wolfram Class IV, e.g. Rule 110; and chaotic-but-structured Class III) maximizes transfer, while trivial (Class I/II) and maximally chaotic rules hurt — so the experiment should sample rules across all four Wolfram classes and treat complexity as the primary independent variable.
- CA spacetime diagrams map cleanly onto the existing ViT pipeline (a fixed N = H×W grid of discrete tokens, frozen random embeddings, masked-token/masked-state prediction), are computationally negligible to generate on the fly, and offer something the formal-grammar tasks do not: genuine 2D local-update structure with both local and long-range (light-cone) dependencies, plus a tunable position on/above the Chomsky hierarchy up to Turing-completeness.

## Key Findings

**1. The parent framework (Shinnick et al., arXiv:2511.13945v2) already anticipates CA.** The paper — "Can You Learn to See Without Images? Procedural Warm-Up for Vision Transformers" by Zachary Shinnick, Liangze Jiang, Hemanth Saratchandran, Damien Teney, and Anton van den Hengel (AIML, University of Adelaide; EPFL; Idiap Research Institute) — pretrains ViTs on procedurally generated symbolic sequences from formal grammars spanning the Chomsky hierarchy: WW (regular), k-Dyck (context-free), and k-Dyck Shuffle (context-sensitive), using masked-token prediction with a vocabulary of 128 tokens (k=64 for the Dyck languages) and frozen random embeddings that bypass the patch embedding. Allocating just 1% of the ImageNet-1K training budget to procedural warm-up improved final accuracy by over 1.7%, equivalent to ~28% of the ImageNet-1K data. Crucially, the related-work section explicitly cites cellular automata as a procedural-data source used in other domains, and the companion language paper (Jiang, Shinnick et al., "Procedural Pretraining: Warming Up Language Models with Abstract Data," arXiv:2601.21725) lists "cellular automata" in its very definition of procedural data. CA is therefore a sanctioned, not speculative, extension axis.

**2. "Intelligence at the Edge of Chaos" is the strongest direct precedent.** Shiyang Zhang (Yale/Columbia), Aakash Patel, Syed A. Rizvi, Sizhuang He, Amin Karbasi and David van Dijk (Yale), Nianchen Liu (Northwestern), and Emanuele Zappala (Idaho State) — arXiv:2410.02536, ICLR 2025 — trained separate GPT-2 models to predict the next state(s) of individual elementary cellular automata (ECA), then froze the backbone and evaluated transfer on reasoning (ARC-inspired) and chess-move-prediction tasks. They found a non-monotonic ("inverted-U") relationship in which, per the abstract, "Both uniform and periodic systems, and often also highly chaotic systems, resulted in poorer downstream performance, highlighting a sweet spot of complexity conducive to intelligence." This is the central empirical motivation for a CA pretraining axis.

**3. Wolfram's four-class taxonomy gives a principled rule-selection scheme.** Of the 256 ECA rules (88 unique under symmetry), canonical class exemplars are: Class I (homogeneous) — Rules 0, 32, 160, 232; Class II (periodic/stable) — Rules 4, 108, 218, 250; Class III (chaotic) — Rules 18, 22, 30, 90, 126, 150, 182; Class IV (complex/edge-of-chaos) — Rules 41, 54, 106, 110, 124, 137, 147, 169, 193, 225. Rule 110 is the canonical Class IV rule and the only ECA with a directly proven Turing-completeness; Rule 30 is the canonical chaotic rule (used as Mathematica's PRNG); Rule 90 generates the Sierpinski triangle; Rule 184 models traffic flow.

**4. CA sits at — and above — the top of the Chomsky hierarchy.** Smith's classical result situates 1D CA in correspondence with linearly bounded automata (context-sensitive, Type-1), but Turing-complete rules (Rule 110; Conway's Game of Life in 2D) make CA capable of universal computation, exceeding context-sensitive recognition and reaching the power of unrestricted (Type-0) grammars / Turing machines. This means a CA axis strictly extends the complexity ladder already used by Shinnick et al. (which topped out at context-sensitive k-Dyck Shuffle).

**5. Masked-state prediction on spacetime diagrams is the natural objective and aligns with masked image modeling.** Stacking successive 1D CA states into a 2D grid (rows = time, columns = space) produces a spacetime diagram with rich, deterministic structural dependencies: each cell is exactly determined by its local neighborhood in the previous row, but information propagates across the grid within a widening "light cone," creating both local and long-range dependencies. This matches the masked-token objective of the parent paper and the masked-image-modeling paradigm — the original Masked Autoencoder (MAE, He et al.) masks 75% of image patches and reconstructs from the remainder, versus BERT's 15% masking rate for text.

## Details

### The parent ViT framework and where CA plugs in
Shinnick et al. propose a lightweight "procedural warm-up" phase for ViTs. Key mechanics that a CA variant must mirror or deliberately vary:
- **Data**: symbolic sequences from formal grammars, generated at negligible cost on the fly.
- **Input path**: bypass the visual patch embedding; map abstract symbols to *random frozen embeddings* that are discarded before image training. Vocabulary of 128 tokens.
- **Shape**: each procedural sample is a fixed-size sequence of N = H×W tokens, matching the ViT's visual-token count.
- **Objective**: masked-token prediction (analogous to BERT/MAE), driving the model to discover the data-generating algorithm.
- **Findings on mechanism**: gains arise from *structured dependencies* in the data (ablations on metamer/shuffled data confirm structure, not statistics, matters); warm-up acts on both attention and MLP layers and disproportionately on *later* layers — contrasting with standard visual pretraining, which acts mostly on early layers, and with structured initializations (Mimetic, CNN-mimicking) that touch only attention. This is evidence the procedural signal is qualitatively distinct and complementary.

The new hypothesis: replace/augment the formal-grammar generator with a CA generator, and replace masked-token prediction over symbol sequences with masked-*state* prediction over CA spacetime configurations.

### "Intelligence at the Edge of Chaos" — setup and quantitative findings
- **Setup**: GPT-2 architecture adapted for binary I/O (token-embedding layer replaced by a linear projection of binary state vectors; deterministic, matching ECA determinism). Each model was pretrained on a single ECA rule for up to 10,000 epochs with a freshly generated dataset each epoch (effectively infinite data). The CA was evolved over 1000 time steps from a random initial condition; training sequences were random spatiotemporal windows of 60 time steps × 100 spatial cells, in batches of 64, using Adam at η = 2×10⁻⁶ with weight decay 0.01 and early stopping; the model predicted 1 or 5 steps into the future. Hardware: 12 NVIDIA H100 GPUs.
- **Downstream evaluation**: backbone frozen; only input/output projections trained. Tasks: easy reasoning, hard reasoning (both ARC-inspired sequence-completion over shapes undergoing color/rotation/shift transforms), and chess move prediction (Lichess Elite database, Grandmaster games ≥2200, SAN notation). Reasoning reported as "efficiency" (1/epochs to reach 80% accuracy); chess reported as final accuracy.
- **Headline result**: a positive correlation between data complexity (Lempel-Ziv) and downstream performance, significant for all tasks. Distance correlations: easy reasoning ≈ 0.72, hard reasoning ≈ 0.44, chess ≈ 0.43 (all p < 0.05). Attention analysis showed models trained on more complex rules attend more to historical states (r = 0.66 between Lempel-Ziv complexity and attention to the last 10 states).
- **Edge of chaos / inverted-U**: Class III and IV (chaotic and complex) rules outperformed Class I and II; Class IV especially excelled on chess. But specific Class III rules — Rules 105, 146, and 150 — underperformed on the harder tasks because, per the paper, "chaotic systems lacking the structured patterns necessary for effective learning... they may be too random to predict, leading to weaker downstream performance." The paper concludes performance "peaks at intermediate complexity and deteriorates with excessive complexity," naming the optimum the "edge of chaos."
- **Representative rules named in text**: Rule 168 (Class I, uniform — consistently low attention), Rule 179 (Class II, periodic — period-2 attention), Rules 105/146/150 (Class III underperformers), Rule 110 (Class IV exemplar, used in scaling experiments).
- **Important caveat for citing this paper**: it trains a *separate model per rule* and evaluates *representation quality of a frozen backbone* — it is not a warm-up-then-finetune vision pipeline, and downstream tasks are symbolic/strategic, not image classification. The transfer claim to ViT image classification is therefore an extrapolation the proposed experiment would test for the first time.

### CA theory relevant to task design
- **Elementary CA (ECA)**: 1D, binary-state, 3-cell neighborhood; 256 rules (an 8-bit lookup table over the 2³=8 neighborhood configurations); 88 unique under reflection/complement symmetries. Rule number = decimal of the 8-bit output column (e.g., 30 = 00011110, 110 = 01101110).
- **Wolfram classes**: I homogeneous; II periodic/stable; III chaotic/aperiodic; IV complex localized structures ("particles"/gliders) — the edge-of-chaos regime conjectured (and for Rule 110 proven) to support universal computation.
- **Turing-completeness**: Rule 110 proven universal by Matthew Cook (2004), via emulation of cyclic tag systems; among the simplest known universal systems (Neary & Woods later gave a polynomial-overhead construction). Conway's Game of Life (2D) is likewise universal. Class IV rules are conjectured generally capable of universal computation (Wolfram's Principle of Computational Equivalence).
- **2D CA / Game of Life**: outer-totalistic (a.k.a. semi-totalistic) binary CA on a 2D square lattice with the 8-cell Moore neighborhood, rule B3/S23 (birth on exactly 3 live neighbors; survival on 2 or 3). 2¹⁸ = 262,144 possible Life-like outer-totalistic rules. "Totalistic" = depends only on neighbor sum; "outer-totalistic" = depends on neighbor sum plus the cell's own state.
- **Neighborhoods / boundaries**: Moore (8) vs. von Neumann (4) neighborhoods; periodic (toroidal), fixed, or reflecting boundary conditions — all are design knobs. LifeGPT (Berkovich & Buehler, npj Artificial Intelligence 2025; arXiv:2409.12182) showed a decoder-only GPT with rotary positional embeddings and forgetful causal masking can learn Game of Life on a toroidal grid topology-agnostically with near-perfect accuracy.
- **Spacetime diagrams**: stacking 1D states over time yields a 2D image; this is the standard CA visualization and the natural ViT input. For 2D CA, a spacetime "diagram" is a 3D volume (x, y, t); a fixed-size 2D ViT input could be a single (x,y) snapshot pair (state t → state t+1) or a flattened window.

### CA vs. neural cellular automata (NCA) — a critical distinction
Neural Cellular Automata (Mordvintsev et al., "Growing Neural Cellular Automata," Distill 2020; and follow-ups on self-organizing textures and self-classifying MNIST) learn a *neural update rule* so a CA *grows/regenerates* a target pattern — i.e., the network *is* the CA. This is the inverse of the proposed task, where a *fixed, known* CA generates data and the ViT learns to *predict masked states* of that data. ViTCA (Tesfaldet et al., NeurIPS 2022) and AdaNCA (2024) hybridize attention with NCA for inpainting/robustness, but again learn-to-be-a-CA rather than learn-from-CA-data. The report should cite NCA only to disambiguate.

### Broader procedural-pretraining and abstract-image literature
- **Formal-language pre-pretraining for LLMs**: Hu, Petty, Shi, Merrill, Linzen, "Between Circuits and Chomsky" (ACL 2025, pp. 9691–9709; arXiv:2502.19249) — pre-pretraining on formal languages that are both hierarchically structured and within transformers' circuit-complexity reach (C-RASP), notably k-Shuffle Dyck, improves natural-language transfer. As the paper states, "For a 1B-parameter language model trained on roughly 1.6B tokens of natural language, pre-pretraining achieves the same loss and better linguistic generalization with a 33% smaller token budget." Ablations show rule-based structure (not n-gram statistics) drives transfer.
- **Synthetic-task pretraining**: LIME (Wu, Rabe, Li, Ba, Grosse, Szegedy, ICML 2021; arXiv:2101.06223) injects deduction/induction/abduction reasoning biases via synthetic tasks, improving math-reasoning benchmarks at small compute. "Insights into Pre-training via Simpler Synthetic Tasks" (Wu, Li, Liang, NeurIPS 2022; arXiv:2206.10139) finds LIME attains ~67% of natural-pretraining benefit, a generic Set task ~65%, and ~39% recoverable from parameter statistics alone.
- **Abstract-image pretraining for vision**: FractalDB / "Pre-training without Natural Images" (Kataoka et al., ACCV 2020 / IJCV 2022; arXiv:2101.08515) — formula-driven supervised learning (FDSL) on fractals; later ExFractalDB-21K reached 83.8% ImageNet-1K top-1 for ViTs. Baradad et al. "Learning to See by Looking at Noise" (NeurIPS 2021; arXiv:2106.05963) and "Procedural Image Programs for Representation Learning" (NeurIPS 2022, shaders21k) show naturalism + diversity of procedural noise drive representation quality, with the most naturalistic data not always best because naturalism can cost diversity. These differ from the proposed work in that they generate abstract *images*; the parent paper and the CA proposal use *non-visual* symbolic structure.
- **Structured initialization**: Mimetic initialization (Trockman & Kolter, ICML 2023; arXiv:2305.09828) sets the product of query and key weights ≈ identity and value-projection ≈ negative identity in closed form, gaining over 5% on CIFAR-10 and over 4% on ImageNet for from-scratch ViTs; it touches only attention. The parent paper distinguishes its method by acting on the whole network and on later layers.

### How CA improves on / complements formal grammars
1. **Higher and tunable position on the complexity ladder**: grammars topped out at context-sensitive; CA reaches Turing-complete (Class IV / Rule 110), and complexity is finely tunable by rule choice across four classes.
2. **Native 2D structure**: spacetime diagrams (and 2D Game of Life) provide genuine spatial 2D dependency structure resembling images more than 1D bracket sequences do, while remaining non-semantic.
3. **Deterministic local-to-global computation**: local update rules create exactly the kind of local + propagating-long-range dependency that convolution-plus-attention architectures must model — arguably a better match to vision than stack-based bracket matching.
4. **Edge-of-chaos as a designed curriculum variable**: the inverted-U finding gives a concrete, falsifiable prediction (Class IV > Class III > Class II > Class I for downstream image accuracy).

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Minimal faithful replication-extension (recommended first).** Keep the entire Shinnick et al. pipeline fixed (frozen random embeddings, N = H×W tokens, masked prediction, identical image-training schedule) and swap only the data generator to ECA spacetime diagrams. Discretize each cell's binary state into the 128-token vocabulary (or use a small vocabulary and pad) so the embedding interface is unchanged. Generate spacetime windows on the fly from random initial conditions. Primary independent variable: Wolfram class of the generating rule. *Benchmark to beat*: the formal-grammar warm-up's +1.7% on ImageNet-1K (and its CIFAR/transfer numbers). If a Class IV CA warm-up matches or exceeds the k-Dyck-Shuffle warm-up at equal compute, the hypothesis is supported.

**Stage 2 — Rule-complexity sweep.** Pretrain across a panel spanning all four classes — e.g., Class I {0, 32, 160}, Class II {108, 250}, Class III {30, 90, 150}, Class IV {54, 110, 137, 193} — plus a "mixed-rule" curriculum. Quantify complexity per rule with Lempel-Ziv (cheap, used by the edge-of-chaos paper) and correlate with downstream image accuracy. *Decision threshold*: if downstream accuracy is non-monotonic in complexity and peaks at Class IV, you have reproduced "edge of chaos" in the vision-warm-up setting — the headline result. If accuracy is flat across classes, the structure is not being exploited and you should revisit the masking scheme.

**Stage 3 — Masking-design ablations.** Test (a) masking ratio (sweep 15%→75%, anchoring on MAE's 75% and BERT's 15%); (b) *which* cells to mask — random vs. "structurally informed" masking of cells whose correct value requires integrating a wide neighborhood/light-cone (forcing long-range reasoning) vs. masking entire future rows (next-state prediction, as in the edge-of-chaos and LifeGPT setups); (c) determinism — because forward CA evolution is deterministic, masking *future* cells yields unambiguous targets (clean signal), whereas masking *past/interior* cells can be ambiguous (multiple pre-images), which may be a feature (harder task) or a problem (noisy loss). Prefer forward/next-state masking first for a clean, well-posed objective.

**Stage 4 — 2D CA / Game of Life and dimensionality.** Add Conway's Game of Life (B3/S23, Moore neighborhood, toroidal boundary) as a 2D source whose (x,y) grid maps directly onto ViT patch geometry, predicting state t+1 from state t. Compare 1D-spacetime vs. 2D-snapshot inputs. *Benchmark*: does native-2D CA beat 1D-spacetime CA, isolating the value of spatial structure?

**Stage 5 — Mechanistic check.** Replicate the parent paper's layerwise/component analysis: confirm whether CA warm-up (like grammar warm-up) acts on later layers and on both attention and MLP, and whether it is complementary (additive) to ImageNet pretraining rather than substitutive. *Threshold*: complementarity (gains persist on top of full ImageNet pretraining and across fine-tuning datasets) is the strongest evidence of a distinct, useful prior.

**What would change the recommendations**: If Stage 1 shows no gain over random init, prioritize the masking redesign (Stage 3) before abandoning the hypothesis — the edge-of-chaos evidence suggests CA *data* carries transferable structure, so a null result more likely indicts the objective/interface than the data. If Stage 2 shows the optimum at Class III rather than IV, that is still a positive (complexity matters) but suggests the ViT benefits from raw entropy more than from "particle" computation, which would refocus rule selection on high-Lempel-Ziv chaotic rules.

## Caveats
- **No prior work directly tests CA-pretraining for ViT image classification.** The edge-of-chaos paper uses GPT-2 backbones, symbolic/strategic downstream tasks, and a frozen-representation probe — not a vision warm-up-then-finetune pipeline. LifeGPT, NCA, and ViTCA are about *being* or *simulating* a CA, not transferring CA-learned priors to natural images. The proposed experiment is genuinely novel; the supporting evidence is by analogy and extrapolation.
- **The edge-of-chaos paper reports no extractable numeric downstream accuracies in text** — chess/reasoning results are plotted in figures, and the random-init baseline is only a dashed line. Cite its correlation coefficients and qualitative inverted-U finding, not specific accuracy percentages.
- **Wolfram-class assignment is qualitative and contested** at the III/IV boundary (Rule 110 has been described as sitting "between Class II and III"); use a quantitative complexity measure (Lempel-Ziv, Lyapunov exponent, compression) as the operational independent variable rather than relying solely on class labels.
- **Determinism cuts both ways**: deterministic forward evolution makes next-state prediction trivially learnable for simple rules (Class I/II), risking a "trivial solution" the model memorizes; the edge-of-chaos paper observed exactly this. The masking scheme must ensure the task remains non-trivial for the chosen rules.
- **Tokenization mismatch**: binary CA states are 2-symbol, whereas the parent pipeline uses a 128-token vocabulary tuned to formal grammars. Coarse-graining several cells into one token (e.g., 7-cell blocks → 128 symbols) or widening neighborhoods/state-counts may be needed to use the vocabulary meaningfully and to create richer local dependencies — itself a design variable.
- **Generation cost is negligible** (CA simulation is cheap, like the grammar generators), so compute is not a constraint on the warm-up phase; the binding constraints are the downstream image-training budget and the breadth of the rule sweep.
